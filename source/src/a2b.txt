I held that opinion because everyone around me seemed to think the same. It was almost taken for granted that success meant a job in Europe or America, and I'd never really questioned it. What changed my mind was a summer exchange programme. I spent two months at a university in Germany. I loved the experience, and I learned a great deal, but I also saw things I hadn't expected. Some of the international students I met were quite lonely, working long hours far from their families. At the same time, I started noticing how much was changing at home, with new companies opening and real opportunities for people with international experience. So my view is now more balanced. I still want to do a master's degree abroad, but I see it as a way of gaining skills to bring back. I feel much calmer about the future than I did.
