## Podcast: Europe by Train | Section 2
@CTX You will hear an episode of a travel podcast in which the presenter gives advice about travelling around Europe by train.
@VOICES Host=f
@S
Host: Hello and welcome back to The Slow Traveller, the podcast for people who think the journey matters as much as the destination. I'm Kate, and this week I'm answering your questions about seeing Europe by train.
Host: The question I get most often is about rail passes. Are they worth it? The honest answer is, it depends. A pass gives you unlimited travel in thirty-three countries, and for people under twenty-eight it's about a quarter cheaper. If you're planning lots of long journeys in a short time, it saves money. But if you only want three or four trips, ordinary tickets booked early are usually cheaper.
Host: And booking early really matters. Tickets for most high-speed trains go on sale about three months ahead, and the lowest prices disappear quickly. One thing people forget is that even with a pass, you often need a seat reservation on fast trains. In France and Spain that's compulsory.
Host: My second tip is to try a night train. They're coming back all over Europe. You fall asleep in Vienna and wake up in Rome, and you save the cost of a hotel. I'd pay a little extra for a bed in a sleeping compartment rather than an ordinary seat. Trust me, your back will thank you.
Host: Third, pack light. You'll be lifting your bag onto racks and carrying it up station stairs. I travel with one backpack of about nine kilos, and I've never wished I had more.
Host: What about food? Station restaurants are expensive. Do what locals do and buy bread, cheese and fruit at a market before you board. A picnic by the window is one of the great pleasures of train travel.
Host: Finally, leave time for connections. I allow at least forty minutes when changing trains, because delays happen, especially in summer.
Host: My favourite route? The line through the Swiss Alps from Chur to Tirano. It crosses nearly two hundred bridges, and every one has a view.
Host: Next week, travelling with children. Until then, enjoy the ride.
