## A Psychology Experiment on Sleep | Section 3
@CTX You will hear two psychology students, Emma and Jack, discussing an experiment they are designing.
@VOICES Emma=f Jack=m
@S

Emma: Yes. I think we should keep it simple. Two groups. Everyone learns a list of word pairs, then one group has a nap and the other stays awake.
Jack: How long should the nap be?
Emma: The studies I read used ninety minutes, but that's too long for volunteers to give up. I'd say twenty minutes.
Jack: Would that be enough to show an effect?
Emma: One paper found an improvement after only six minutes, so I think so.
Jack: OK. And what does the other group do during that time?

Jack: Good idea. How many participants do we need?
Emma: Doctor Lewis said at least forty, so twenty in each group.
Jack: We should put them in groups at random, not let them choose. Otherwise all the tired people will choose the nap.
Emma: Exactly. We could toss a coin for each person.
Jack: Where can people sleep, though? The psychology lab is so noisy.
Emma: I asked about that. We can use the quiet room in the library, as long as we book it a week ahead.
Jack: Great. And what time of day?
Emma: Early afternoon, about two o'clock. That's when people naturally feel sleepy.
Jack: Should we test them straight after the nap?
Emma: I'd wait ten minutes, so they're properly awake. People are often confused just after waking.
Jack: Fine. What about the ethics form?
Emma: We must tell participants not to drive straight afterwards, in case they still feel sleepy. And nobody under eighteen can take part.
Jack: I'll write the method section if you do the introduction.
Emma: Deal. Send me your part by Saturday.
