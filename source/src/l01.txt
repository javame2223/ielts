## A Tour of the City Museum | Section 2
@CTX You will hear a guide welcoming visitors to a city museum.
@VOICES Guide=f
@S
Guide: Good afternoon, everyone, and welcome to the City Museum. Before you start exploring, I'd like to tell you a little about the building and what you can see today.
Guide: The museum opened in eighteen ninety-two. The building was originally a wool warehouse, which explains the large windows and high ceilings. It was converted into a museum after a local businessman left his private collection to the city.
Guide: We're now standing in the entrance hall on the ground floor. To your left is the Local History gallery, which tells the story of the town from Roman times to the present. The most popular exhibit there is a model of the old harbour as it looked three hundred years ago.
Guide: On the first floor you'll find the Natural World gallery. Children love the dinosaur skeleton, which is over nine metres long. Next to it is our temporary exhibition. This month it's about the history of photography, and it finishes on the thirtieth of June.
Guide: The second floor has the art collection, mainly paintings of the sea and the coast by local artists. Please note that photography is allowed everywhere in the museum except in the art gallery.
Guide: If you're hungry, the café is in the basement and it serves hot food until half past two. The gift shop is beside the main exit.
Guide: We have two free talks today. At two o'clock there's a talk about Roman coins in the Local History gallery, and at three thirty a member of staff will show how old clocks are repaired. For the second one you need a ticket from the information desk, because there are only twenty places.
Guide: Finally, the museum closes at five thirty, and the last entry to the galleries is at five. Lockers for bags are next to the stairs. You'll need a one pound coin, which you get back afterwards. Enjoy your visit.

## Volunteering at the Summer Festival | Section 2
@CTX You will hear an organiser giving information to people who want to work as volunteers at a town festival.
@VOICES Organiser=f
@S
Organiser: Thanks for coming this evening. I'm Helen, and I coordinate the volunteers for the Greenfield Summer Festival.
Organiser: As most of you know, the festival takes place in Victoria Park over three days, from Friday the eighth of July to Sunday the tenth. Last year we had around twelve thousand visitors, and this year we're expecting even more, so we need about eighty volunteers.
Organiser: There are three main jobs. The first is working at the entrance gates, checking tickets and giving out maps. The second is helping in the children's area, where there'll be face painting, games and a small theatre. For that job we must check your background, so please complete the form tonight. The third job is the green team, which looks after recycling. It's not glamorous, but last year we recycled seventy per cent of all the waste, and we want to do better.
Organiser: Each shift lasts five hours, and we ask everybody to do at least two shifts over the weekend. In return you get a free T-shirt, a meal for each shift and a free ticket for one of the days when you're not working.
Organiser: There's a training session for all volunteers on Saturday the second of July at ten in the morning. It's in the community centre, not in the park, and it takes about two hours. Please wear comfortable shoes on the day, because you'll be standing a lot, and bring a waterproof jacket. You know what the weather is like here.
Organiser: If you're driving, you can't park at the main entrance. Use the car park in Mill Lane, which is reserved for staff. Show your volunteer badge.
Organiser: If you have any problems during the festival, the volunteer office will be in the white tent beside the main stage. My mobile number is on the information sheet. Now, are there any questions?

## Changes to the Public Library | Section 2
@CTX You will hear a radio announcement about changes at a town library.
@VOICES Presenter=m Manager=f
@S
Presenter: And now some local news. Listeners who use Kingsbridge Library will be pleased to hear that it reopens next Monday after being closed for four months for rebuilding. The library's manager, Sarah Collins, is here to tell us what's new.
Manager: Thank you. Yes, we're very excited. The biggest change is that the library is now on two floors instead of one. Downstairs is the children's library, which is twice as big as before, with a special corner for story time. The adult books have moved upstairs, and there's a lift for anybody who can't use the stairs.
Manager: We've also created a quiet study room with thirty desks. Students asked for this for years. Each desk has a lamp and a socket for a laptop. You can reserve a desk online for up to three hours at a time.
Manager: The old computer room has become a café. It's run by a local bakery, and you're welcome to take your drinks to the reading area, but not into the study room, please.
Manager: Our opening hours are longer. We'll be open from nine in the morning to eight in the evening from Monday to Friday, and from ten to four on Saturdays. We're still closed on Sundays.
Manager: Borrowing is easier too. You can now take out twelve books instead of eight, and keep them for four weeks. There are no more fines for children's books returned late.
Manager: To celebrate, we're holding an open day on Saturday the fourteenth. A well-known crime writer, James Porter, will be reading from his new novel at eleven o'clock, and in the afternoon there's a workshop on tracing your family history. Both are free, but please book by phone, because space is limited.
Presenter: Sarah, thank you. That's Kingsbridge Library, reopening on Monday.
